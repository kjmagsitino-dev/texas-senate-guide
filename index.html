U.S. Senate Race for Texas · General Election · November 3, 2026

A nonpartisan guide to the 2026 Texas Senate race.

For every Texas voter.

This guide covers the U.S. Senate race only. For governor and other races on your ballot, see Vote411.org.

TL;DR

🏟️

2 major-party candidates. One U.S. Senate seat. Election Day: November 3, 2026.

🏛️

This is a federal race. The winner goes to Washington — not Austin. They vote on judges, healthcare, immigration, and the national budget.

🗳️

In the last Texas Senate race, about 7 million registered Texans didn't vote — more than 1 in 3 registered voters sitting it out.

What This Race Does — and Doesn't — Control

A U.S. Senator is one of 100 members of the Senate. New federal laws need a majority in both the Senate and the House, plus the President's signature. They don't run Texas day-to-day — but what Washington decides sets the rules every state operates within.

✓ What a U.S. Senator votes on

  • 💊Prescription drug prices. Federal law shapes what Medicare and other programs pay for drugs. A 2022 federal law let Medicare negotiate prices directly with drug makers for the first time, for a limited set of drugs.
  • 🏥Medicare and Medicaid. Medicare covers most Americans 65+; Medicaid covers low-income adults and children. Congress sets federal funding for both (Medicaid is shared with states) — cuts or increases in Washington directly affect Texans enrolled.
  • 🌐Immigration and the border. The border is a federal responsibility. Senators vote on immigration laws, enforcement funding, and legal pathways for undocumented immigrants.
  • ⚖️Federal judges and the Supreme Court. The Senate confirms the President's nominees for federal judgeships, including Supreme Court justices — whose decisions shape abortion rights, gun laws, and voting rules nationwide.
  • 💰Federal taxes and the budget. Senators vote on tax rates, how much the government spends, Social Security funding, and the national debt.
  • 🔫Federal gun laws. Congress sets the national baseline — like who needs a background check. States can add stricter rules but can't go below the federal minimum.

✗ What stays at the state or local level

  • 🏠Property taxes. Set by your county and local government. A U.S. Senator has no direct control over your property tax bill.
  • 🏫Most public school funding. Most Texas school funding comes from state and local sources. The federal share was 13.7% in 2023–24 (the U.S. average was 11.7%), boosted by pandemic relief aid. A Senator influences only that federal share.
  • 🚦Texas state laws. Laws like Texas's abortion ban are passed in Austin, not Washington. That said, federal laws and Supreme Court rulings can affect what states are allowed to do.
  • ⛽Gas and grocery prices. Mainly driven by global markets, supply chains, and the Federal Reserve. Federal energy and trade policy can play a role, but no single Senate vote sets prices.

⚡ Energy & Environment
Supports wind, solar, oil, and gas
Calls for an "all-of-the-above" approach. He wants federal tax credits and loans for wind, solar, and geothermal, and he backs oil and gas jobs. He also wants stronger Clean Air and Clean Water Acts. In 2021 he filed the Texas Climate Action Act, a bill to cut emissions.
Supports fewer energy rules
His campaign criticizes Biden for "declaring war on American energy" and promises to address "harmful regulations designed to hurt our energy companies." As AG, he sued BlackRock, State Street, and Vanguard over their moves away from coal. He also opposed Biden-era federal climate rules.
💰 Federal Budget
Wants higher taxes on the wealthy
Wants higher taxes on billionaires and large corporations, and to end the cap on Social Security taxes for income above $400,000. Has called the national debt "intergenerational theft." Opposes cuts to Social Security, Medicare, and Medicaid.
Wants less spending and lower taxes
Pledges to "stop the wasteful spending" and cut taxes. In a leaked recording of a Sept. 24 private lunch (reported Oct. 8), he said "reasonable cuts to mandatory spending" would be needed. That term covers programs like Social Security and Medicare. He did not name programs. He later called Social Security "a sacred promise," denied plans to take away earned benefits, and called the audio "cherry-picked."
🏫 Education
Opposes school vouchers
Opposes using public money for private school tuition. Helped pass a major school funding bill in 2019. His House biography also credits him with the first-ever cap on Pre-K class sizes.
Supports school vouchers
As AG, he celebrated Texas's 2025 school choice law and vowed to defend it. His campaign pledges to "protect parental rights" and opposes "woke indoctrination in our classrooms." He has also backed the state law that requires the Ten Commandments in public school classrooms.
💵 Economy
Wants a middle-class tax cut
Proposes a middle-class tax cut: larger child and earned-income tax credits, restored ACA credits, and a tariff rebate. He would pay for it by taxing the wealthiest and large corporations more. Wants to curb corporate landlords and expand the tax credit for affordable housing. Has pledged to take no money from corporate political action committees (PACs).
Wants new tax deductions and credits
His July 2026 tax plan adds deductions: up to $25,000 for medical costs, $50,000 for first-time homebuyers, and $5,000 for things like gym memberships and GLP-1 medications. It also doubles the child tax credit, to up to $4,400 per child. He praised the 2025 Republican tax law that made the 2017 tax cuts permanent, and says "America must be the crypto capital of the world."
🏥 Healthcare
Supports the ACA and Medicaid
Wants to protect and strengthen the Affordable Care Act and restore its larger tax credits. Wants to protect Medicaid from federal cuts and let anyone buy into Medicare as a not-for-profit option. Co-led the 2021 Texas law that capped insulin at $25 a month (credit is disputed; see Their Record). Opposes cuts to Medicare and Social Security.
Opposes the ACA
As AG, he repeatedly challenged the Affordable Care Act in court and has long opposed the law. His campaign pledges to take on "Big Pharma and Big Food" and to increase transparency in healthcare. His campaign site does not address the expiring larger ACA tax credits.
🏳️‍🌈 LGBTQ+ Issues
Opposes broad bans; backs some limits
Opposed the 2021 transgender sports bill and voted against SB 14 (2023), which barred puberty blockers, hormones, and surgery for minors. He says SB 14 was too broad. He now supports barring transition surgery for minors. He also opposes transgender athletes in girls' sports when safety or fairness is at issue. For his past comments, see Controversies.
Opposes expanding protections
His campaign vows to "defeat the radical transgender movement" and opposes LGBTQ-focused content in schools. As AG, he has challenged Obama- and Biden-era LGBTQ+ policies.
🔫 Gun Policy
Supports more restrictions
Voted against Texas's law that lets most adults carry handguns without a permit. His campaign site backs background checks on all gun sales, a higher age to buy semi-automatic rifles with military-style features, and safe storage around children. In a September 2026 TV clip, asked if he would vote for a ban on assault-style weapons and high-capacity magazines, he said "Absolutely."
Opposes new restrictions
His campaign pledges to "protect our gun rights." He has condemned the Bipartisan Safer Communities Act (2022) as infringing on Second Amendment rights.
🌐 Immigration & Border
Supports a citizenship path and enforcement
Wants a path to citizenship for the longest-resident immigrants, including Dreamers and spouses, and temporary work permits for recent arrivals who fill needed jobs. Would prioritize deporting criminals, gang members, and human traffickers. Would add Border Patrol agents and scanning technology at ports of entry, and ban ICE agents from wearing masks.
Supports stricter enforcement
Backs President Trump on finishing the border wall and deporting "criminal illegal aliens." As AG, his office sued the Biden administration to end DACA (protection for people brought here as children) and catch-and-release (letting detained migrants go while their cases continue), and to keep building the border wall. These were among 100+ lawsuits against the administration.
🤰 Abortion
Supports abortion access
Voted against Texas's near-total abortion ban and calls it an "extreme abortion ban" with dangerous outcomes for women in medical emergencies. Supports abortion rights and wants to write Roe v. Wade into federal law. Says abortion should be treated as a public-health issue, not a culture-war one.
Supports abortion restrictions
Supports Texas's near-total abortion ban and defended it as AG. The law says an "unborn child" begins at fertilization. He sued cities that tried to fund abortion travel for residents. His campaign says he will "defend the unborn." States Newsroom reports he has recently indicated openness to restrictions on IVF.

Why This Race Matters

Four things worth knowing before November.

01
📊

This race is a toss-up, and polls are very close

As of Oct. 20, 2026Ratings: Cook Political Report rates the race a toss-up, meaning either candidate could win. Fox News Power Rankings moved it to toss-up in August.

Polls: Recent polls are very close. Most show Talarico ahead by 2 to 6 points, such as CBS News/YouGov 51%–49% (Oct. 8–16), University of Houston 48%–46% (reported Oct. 19), and Marist 47%–45% (Oct. 8–11). At least two show Paxton ahead by a point or less: InsiderAdvantage 49%–48% and Quantus Insights 47.2%–46.7%.
